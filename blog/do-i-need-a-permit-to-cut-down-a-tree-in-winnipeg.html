Do I Need a Permit to Cut Down a Tree in Winnipeg? Your Complete Guide to Tree Removal Rules and Timing

Every spring and fall, Winnipeg homeowners stare up at a leaning oak, a dead Manitoba maple, or an elm rubbing against the eaves and ask the same question: can I just cut this thing down, or do I need to jump through bureaucratic hoops first? The answer depends on where the tree is, what species it is, and when you plan to work — and getting it wrong can cost you more than the removal itself. Here is what you need to know before you call an arborist or pick up a chainsaw.

Do I Need a Permit to Cut Down a Tree in Winnipeg?

For a tree located entirely on private property in Winnipeg, you generally do not need a permit to remove it — but there are important exceptions. Trees on a boulevard (the grass strip between the sidewalk and the street) are City of Winnipeg property, and you absolutely need approval before touching them. Additionally, elm trees anywhere in the city are protected under Manitoba's Dutch Elm Disease management rules, which strictly regulate pruning windows and removal procedures regardless of who owns the land. If you live in a heritage neighbourhood or a development with a tree-preservation covenant, additional restrictions may apply to your lot.

Regulatory Note: The City of Winnipeg owns and manages all boulevard trees. Cutting, pruning, or removing a boulevard tree without authorization is a bylaw violation and can result in significant fines. If you are unsure whether a tree sits on your property or the boulevard, check your property survey or contact 311 before proceeding.

What Are Winnipeg's Rules for Elm Trees Specifically?

Elm trees in Winnipeg have their own strict rules because of Dutch Elm Disease (DED), a fungal infection spread by elm bark beetles. Provincial and municipal regulations prohibit pruning or wounding elm trees between April 1 and July 31 each year — this is the period when bark beetles are most active and the risk of spreading the disease is highest. Removal of a dead or dying elm is permitted year-round, but the wood must be disposed of properly; you cannot leave elm wood on your property or haul it to a landfill that does not accept it, as the infected material can still spread the disease. For full details on the provincial program, see the Manitoba Government — Dutch Elm Disease Program.

Important: Pruning a healthy elm tree between April 1 and July 31 is illegal under Manitoba regulations. Even well-meaning pruning during this window can attract bark beetles and expose the tree — and neighbouring elms — to Dutch Elm Disease.

When Is the Best Time of Year to Remove or Trim Trees in Manitoba?

The best time to remove or trim most trees in Winnipeg is late fall through early spring — roughly November through March — when trees are dormant, the ground is frozen, and insects are inactive. Dormant-season removal is safer for the tree's neighbours, easier for crews to access the site with heavy equipment over frozen ground, and often less disruptive to your yard since there are no leaves to manage. For elms specifically, work must be completed before April 1 or deferred until after July 31.

Spring (April through early June) is popular for ornamental pruning of species like mountain ash, linden, and fruit trees once the elm restriction window is clear, but this is also when arborists in Winnipeg are busiest — booking several weeks ahead is the norm. Summer removal is practical for identifying dead wood (it stands out clearly against green foliage) but heat and insect pressure make it harder on both crew and equipment. Fall — September through October — is a sweet spot: the weather is cooperative, schedules open up, and you can assess which trees did not survive the summer before winter sets in.

What Happens If the Tree Is on the Boulevard or Near a Property Line?

Boulevard trees belong to the City of Winnipeg, not to the homeowner adjacent to them. You cannot remove, prune, or even spike a boulevard tree without city authorization — doing so is a bylaw violation. If a boulevard tree is damaged, diseased, or poses a safety risk, report it to the City of Winnipeg through 311 and they will dispatch Urban Forestry to assess it. For trees near a property line that straddle two lots, both property owners technically have a stake in the tree, and removal generally requires agreement from both parties — a situation worth clarifying with the city or a legal professional before work begins.

How Do I Know If My Tree Needs Removal vs. Pruning?

A certified arborist is the right person to make this call, but some signs strongly suggest removal is necessary rather than pruning: more than 50 percent of the crown is dead or dying, the trunk has significant decay or large fungal conks at the base, the tree is leaning toward a structure and the lean has increased recently, or root damage from construction or frost heave has destabilized the tree. In Winnipeg's climate — with freeze-thaw cycles that stress root systems and heavy wet snowfalls that split weak branch unions — trees that might survive in a milder climate often reach the end of their safe lifespan sooner. Frequently Asked Questions

Can I remove a tree on my Winnipeg property without hiring a professional?

Legally, there is no rule in Winnipeg that requires you to hire a certified arborist for a tree on your own private property — but it is strongly recommended for any tree taller than about 20 feet or near a structure, fence, or utility line. DIY removal of a large tree is one of the most common causes of serious property damage and personal injury for homeowners, and most home insurance policies will not cover self-inflicted damage from an untrained removal attempt.

Is there a fine for cutting down a boulevard tree in Winnipeg without permission?

Yes — unauthorized removal or damage to a City of Winnipeg boulevard tree can result in fines, and you may also be required to pay the replacement cost of the tree, which the city calculates based on the tree's assessed value. Large mature boulevard elms or oaks can carry assessed replacement values in the thousands of dollars. Always contact 311 first if you believe a boulevard tree is hazardous.

Are there any tree species in Manitoba that are protected and cannot be removed from private property?

Manitoba does not have a broad provincial list of protected tree species that prevents private landowners from removing trees on their own lots, but elm trees are subject to strict management rules under the Dutch Elm Disease program that effectively regulate how and when they can be handled. Some municipalities and rural municipalities may have their own tree-protection bylaws, so if you are outside Winnipeg city limits, check with your local authority before removing any large tree.

How far in advance should I book a tree removal in Winnipeg?

For spring removals (April–June), book at least three to five weeks in advance because this is peak season and most reputable arborists fill up quickly after the snow melts. For fall and winter work, one to two weeks is usually sufficient, and you may even find same-week availability in January or February. Emergency removals after storm damage are handled on a priority basis regardless of season.

What should I do with the wood and stump after a tree is removed in Winnipeg?

For non-elm wood, you can keep it for firewood, have it chipped into mulch on-site, or arrange pickup through the City of Winnipeg's bulk waste program — confirm current guidelines with 311 as collection schedules vary by neighbourhood. Elm wood must be disposed of at a facility approved to handle DED-infected material and cannot be stockpiled on your property or transported freely; your removal contractor should handle compliant elm wood disposal as part of the job. Stumps require a separate grinding service and are not automatically included in most tree removal quotes, so confirm this upfront.
