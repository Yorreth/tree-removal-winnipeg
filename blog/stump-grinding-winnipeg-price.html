Stump Grinding Winnipeg Price Guide: What to Expect in 2026

If a tree crew just finished removing a tree from your yard in River Heights, Transcona, or anywhere else in Winnipeg, you may be staring at a stump and wondering what it will cost to get rid of it. Stump grinding is the most common and practical removal method used by Winnipeg arborists, and knowing the local price range before you call for quotes will help you budget accurately and avoid overpaying.

How Much Does Stump Grinding Cost in Winnipeg?

Most Winnipeg homeowners pay between $100 and $400 per stump for stump grinding, with the typical single-stump job landing around $150 to $225. Larger stumps from mature elm or cottonwood trees — common in older Winnipeg neighbourhoods like Wolseley, St. Boniface, and the West End — can push costs toward $350 or more depending on diameter and root spread. If you have multiple stumps on the same property, most local contractors offer a per-inch or flat-rate discount that can meaningfully reduce the per-stump cost.

What Factors Affect the Stump Grinding Price in Winnipeg?

Stump Diameter

The single biggest pricing variable is the diameter of the stump measured at ground level. Many Winnipeg grinding contractors charge a base fee (often $75–$100) that covers the first 6–12 inches, then add $3–$6 per additional inch of diameter. A 24-inch elm stump could therefore cost $150–$200 in diameter charges alone before any other factors apply.

Tree Species and Root Hardness

Manitoba is home to a wide range of hardwood trees — green ash, Manitoba maple, American elm, and bur oak are all common in Winnipeg yards. Hardwood stumps dull grinding equipment faster and take longer to chew through. Expect to pay a small premium — often 10–20% more — for dense hardwoods compared to softer species like poplar or white spruce.

Stump Age and Condition

Fresh stumps are generally harder to grind than older, partially decayed ones. If a stump has been sitting in your yard for several winters (Winnipeg's freeze-thaw cycles tend to accelerate decay), the job may be quicker and slightly cheaper. Conversely, a newly cut mature bur oak will take longer and cost more than an equivalent-sized poplar stump that's been rotting for three years.

Accessibility and Site Conditions

Grinding equipment needs to reach the stump. If your stump is behind a fence, down a narrow side yard, or on a slope, the contractor may need a compact grinder rather than a full-sized machine — sometimes adding $50–$100 to the quote. If you're planning a new fence after tree removal, it's worth coordinating the stump grinding first so posts can be set cleanly; homeowners in this situation sometimes bundle services and reach out to a Fence Installation Winnipeg contractor shortly after.

Grinding Depth

Standard grinding goes 6–8 inches below grade, which is enough to sod or replant over. If you need deeper grinding — say, 12 inches or more — for a patio, deck, or new tree planting, expect to pay 20–30% more. Be sure to clarify depth requirements before signing off on a quote.

Debris Removal

Stump grinding produces a significant pile of wood chips. Many Winnipeg contractors leave the chips on site (they make decent mulch), but hauling them away typically adds $50–$150 to the invoice. If you have garden beds that could use fresh mulch, ask the crew to pile the grindings there — it's free material that would otherwise cost you at the garden centre.

Does Winnipeg's Climate Affect When You Can Get Stump Grinding Done?

Yes — stump grinding in Winnipeg is a seasonal service, and timing affects both availability and price. Most grinding contractors operate from late April through October, once the ground has thawed enough to safely work around roots. Prices tend to be most competitive in early spring (before the summer rush) and in September, after the peak demand of summer has eased. Trying to book in July or August — when every homeowner in Tuxedo and Charleswood seems to want their yard cleaned up — often means longer waits and occasionally higher quotes. Some contractors do offer winter grinding, but it is rare, costs significantly more, and the frozen ground makes cleanup difficult.

If the stump you need ground is from a tree that was on or near a city boulevard, check with the City of Winnipeg — Trees, Boulevards & Urban Forestry department before proceeding. Boulevard trees are city property, and work on them — including stump removal — typically requires City approval or is carried out by City crews rather than private contractors.

How to Get the Best Price on Stump Grinding in Winnipeg

  • Get at least three quotes. Pricing among Winnipeg grinding contractors varies more than you might expect. A five-minute call to three different companies can save you $75 or more on a single stump.
  • Bundle with tree removal. If you're having a tree taken down at the same time, ask for a combined quote. Our stump grinding service is frequently booked together with full tree removal, and the savings can be meaningful.
  • Ask neighbours to split a trip charge. If your neighbour on your street in East Kildonan or Fort Garry also has a stump to deal with, sharing a contractor visit can reduce per-stump costs by eliminating duplicate travel and setup fees.
  • Book in the shoulder season. Late April or September bookings often come with better availability and sometimes better pricing than peak summer slots.
  • Leave the chips. Declining debris hauling is an easy way to trim $50–$150 from your invoice if you have any garden beds or areas that could use organic mulch.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is stump grinding cheaper than full stump removal in Winnipeg?

Yes, stump grinding is almost always significantly cheaper than full stump extraction in Winnipeg. Grinding typically costs $100–$400 per stump, while full extraction — which involves digging out the entire root ball — can run $300–$800 or more and often requires larger equipment and more labour. For most residential yards in Winnipeg, grinding is the practical and cost-effective choice.

Will stump grinding kill the roots so the tree doesn't regrow?

Standard stump grinding eliminates the stump and surface roots but does not chemically kill every underground root, so some tree species — particularly Manitoba maple and green ash, which are prolific in Winnipeg — can send up new shoots from remaining root tissue. Applying a stump treatment or herbicide to the freshly ground area significantly reduces regrowth; ask your contractor whether this is included or available as an add-on.

How long does stump grinding take for an average Winnipeg residential property?

A single average-sized stump (12–20 inches in diameter) typically takes 30 to 60 minutes to grind on a standard Winnipeg residential lot, once the equipment is on site. Larger stumps or those with significant surface root systems can take 1.5 to 2 hours. Most contractors include cleanup and backfilling of the hole with chips in that time estimate.

Can I plant a new tree in the same spot after stump grinding?

You can plant a new tree in the same area after stump grinding, but it is best to wait at least one growing season and to remove as much of the wood chip debris from the hole as possible, replacing it with fresh topsoil. Decomposing wood chips temporarily tie up soil nitrogen, which can stress a newly planted tree. Many Winnipeg arborists recommend offsetting the new planting by a few feet from the original stump location to give the roots a cleaner start.
