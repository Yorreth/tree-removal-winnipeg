Tree Removal Winnipeg Cost: What Homeowners Need to Know Before They Book

If a dying elm is looming over your River Heights bungalow or a storm-damaged Manitoba maple is threatening the fence in your St. Vital yard, the first question on your mind is almost certainly the same: what is this going to cost me? Tree removal winnipeg cost varies more than most homeowners expect, shaped by the size and species of the tree, how accessible it is, what time of year you book, and — crucially — whether you need a permit before anyone picks up a chainsaw. This guide breaks down all of those factors in plain language so you can plan your budget and avoid surprises.

Typical Price Ranges for Tree Removal in Winnipeg

Winnipeg arborists generally price jobs by the hour or by the tree, depending on complexity. The table below reflects what local companies are quoting in 2026 for residential work. These are ballpark ranges — always get at least two or three written estimates for your specific situation.

Winnipeg-Specific Factors That Affect the Price

Dutch Elm Disease and the Elm Pruning Ban

Winnipeg is home to one of the largest urban elm canopies in North America, and the city takes protecting it seriously. Work on elm trees is tightly regulated, and there is a seasonal pruning ban in place each year to limit the spread of Dutch Elm Disease.

Important: The City of Winnipeg enforces an annual elm pruning ban, typically running from approximately April 1 through July 31. During this period, pruning or wounding elm trees — including removal work that exposes the wood — is prohibited. Violating this ban can result in fines. Before booking any elm work, confirm current dates with the City of Winnipeg — Trees, Boulevards & Urban Forestry. Arborists who schedule elm removal outside the ban window may charge a modest premium for the narrow booking period.

Access and Lot Constraints

Older Winnipeg neighbourhoods like Wolseley, St. Boniface, and the West End often have narrow side yards, mature gardens, and tight fences that make it impossible to bring in heavy equipment. In those cases, crews must climb and hand-cut, which takes longer and costs more. Conversely, newer areas like Sage Creek or Bridgwater with wider lots and open access can bring in cranes or large chippers, keeping labour hours down.

Proximity to Power Lines and Structures

Trees growing into or near overhead hydro lines in Winnipeg require coordination with Manitoba Hydro or a certified utility arborist. This adds both cost and scheduling delays. If your tree is touching or close to lines, expect quotes at the higher end of the range and plan for a longer lead time.

Seasonal Timing

Winter is often the most cost-effective time to have trees removed in Winnipeg. Frozen ground protects your lawn from equipment damage, and many arborists offer off-season discounts to keep crews busy during slower months. Late spring and summer — peak storm season on the Prairies — typically see the highest demand and the longest waits. Booking in October or November, after the elm ban lifts and before deep freeze, is a sweet spot many local homeowners miss.

Do You Need a Permit to Remove a Tree in Winnipeg?

Permit Requirements: The City of Winnipeg has regulations governing the removal of trees on public boulevards and in certain protected areas. Trees on private property may also be subject to local bylaws depending on their size, species, or location near a water course or park. Before removing any significant tree, check with the City of Winnipeg to determine whether a permit or approval is required. Removing a protected tree without authorization can result in fines and a requirement to replant. Your arborist should be familiar with local rules, but confirming independently is always a good idea.

Boulevard trees — the trees between the sidewalk and the street — are considered city property regardless of which yard they appear to be in. You cannot remove or significantly prune a boulevard tree without city authorization. Many homeowners in Crescentwood and Charleswood have been caught off guard by this rule when a tree they assumed was theirs turned out to be city-owned.

What Is and Is Not Included in a Typical Quote

  • Usually included: Felling, limbing, sectioning, and chipping of branches and debris removal from the site.
  • Often extra: Stump grinding, log splitting or firewood prep, hauling away large trunk sections.
  • Your responsibility to confirm: Whether the arborist carries liability insurance and WorkSafe Manitoba coverage — always ask for proof before work begins.

Storm Damage: An Unpredictable Cost Driver

Winnipeg's weather is hard on trees. Ice storms in early spring and severe thunderstorms through July and August regularly split large cottonwoods and ash trees, sometimes dropping branches onto roofs, fences, and vehicles. Emergency removal after storm damage costs significantly more than planned removal — emergency call-out fees and after-hours labour can push a job that would normally cost $800 into the $1,500–$2,500 range. If a storm has damaged both your trees and your roofline, it is worth noting that falling branches frequently damage gutters and downspouts as well — a local specialist like Eavestrough Winnipeg can assess and repair gutter damage alongside your tree work to prevent water infiltration before winter sets in.

Getting the Best Value on Tree Removal in Winnipeg

  1. Get a minimum of three written quotes from ISA-certified arborists.
  2. Book outside peak season — late fall and winter offer the best rates.
  3. Bundle multiple trees if you have more than one that needs attention; arborists often discount multi-tree jobs.
  4. Confirm permit requirements with the City before signing anything.
  5. Ask whether the company handles boulevard tree coordination directly or whether you need to apply separately.
