Tree Removal in Winnipeg: A Step-by-Step Guide to What Happens on the Job

Know exactly what to expect before, during, and after the crew shows up at your property.

Booking a tree removal in Winnipeg can feel overwhelming if you have never been through the process before. Whether you are dealing with a storm-damaged elm on Inkster Boulevard or a dead spruce crowding your fence line in St. Vital, understanding exactly what the job involves helps you prepare your property, ask the right questions, and avoid surprises on the day. This guide walks you through the full process, from the moment the crew pulls up to your driveway to the final rake of the yard.

Step 1: Site Assessment and Safety Planning

Before a single branch comes down, a qualified arborist or crew lead walks the site. They are evaluating several things at once: the lean and weight distribution of the tree, proximity to your home, power lines, fences, and neighbouring properties. In Winnipeg, overhead utility lines are an especially common complication, and Manitoba Hydro regulations require that only certified line-clearance arborists work within a certain distance of energized conductors. If your tree is close to a power line, expect the company to coordinate with Manitoba Hydro before the job proceeds.

The crew will also determine the felling direction and plan escape routes for ground workers. In tighter city lots — common in older Winnipeg neighbourhoods like Wolseley, Point Douglas, or Transcona — a straight fell is rarely possible, so sectional removal from the top down becomes the standard approach.

Step 2: Setting Up the Work Zone

Once the plan is confirmed, the crew establishes a clear perimeter. Cones, caution tape, or simple verbal agreements with neighbours create a drop zone. Equipment is positioned strategically: a chipper is typically parked close to the curb, and a bucket truck or lift — if needed — is staged on a firm surface to avoid damaging your lawn. If your property has a narrow gate or limited driveway access, communicate this before the crew arrives so they can bring the right equipment.

This is also the time to move vehicles, patio furniture, and any fragile landscaping features out of the work area. A few minutes of preparation on your part saves potential headaches later.

Step 3: Removal — Sectional or Straight Fell

For most residential tree removal in Winnipeg, the crew works from the top down. A climber ascends using ropes and harnesses, or the team uses an aerial lift, and removes the tree in manageable sections. Each limb or section is either lowered with rigging ropes to control descent or dropped into a clear zone. Sections are then fed through the chipper or cut into rounds for firewood, depending on your arrangement with the company.

If the tree is in an open area — a rural acreage near Headingley or a large lot with clear drop zones — a straight fell may be used, which is faster and less labour-intensive. The arborist will make a directional notch cut on the fall side and a back cut to guide the tree precisely where they want it to land.

Step 4: Wood Disposal and Chipping

Branches and smaller material are typically fed through a wood chipper on-site. The resulting chips can be left as a mulch pile on your property at your request — a useful option for garden beds — or hauled away by the crew. Larger trunk sections are cut into rounds. Some Winnipeg homeowners keep these for firewood, particularly for ash or oak. Confirm your preferences in advance so the crew knows whether to haul everything away or leave material behind.

Step 5: Stump Grinding

Once the tree is down, you are left with a stump. Most professional tree removal services in Winnipeg offer stump grinding as either an included service or an add-on. A stump grinder uses a rotating cutting wheel to reduce the stump and surface roots to wood chips a few inches below grade. This allows you to re-seed or sod over the area. Full stump removal — pulling the entire root ball — is far more invasive and rarely necessary for residential properties.

If your property is near a fence line, note that stump grinding equipment needs adequate clearance to maneuver. Storm-damaged trees that have also shifted or cracked a fence may require fence repairs alongside the job, which is worth discussing with a contractor who handles Fence Installation Winnipeg projects.

Step 6: Final Cleanup

A reputable tree removal crew leaves your property clean. Expect them to rake and blow the work area, remove all wood debris unless you have agreed to keep some, and do a final walkthrough with you to confirm satisfaction. Small wood chips may settle into grass and are generally harmless to lawns.

Typical Cost Ranges for Tree Removal in Winnipeg

Costs vary considerably depending on tree size, species, location, and whether stump grinding is included. Below is a general reference for Winnipeg homeowners.

Tree Size Estimated Cost (CAD) Notes
Small (under 20 ft) $250 – $500 Shrubs, ornamental trees, young elms
Medium (20–50 ft) $500 – $1,200 Mature spruce, ash, linden
Large (50–80 ft) $1,200 – $2,500 Large elm, cottonwood, Manitoba maple
Extra Large (80 ft+) $2,500 – $5,000+ Old-growth elms, hazard trees near structures
Stump Grinding (add-on) $100 – $400 Depends on diameter and root spread

These figures are estimates. Always request a written quote specific to your tree and property conditions before committing to any contractor.

What to Ask Before You Book

  • Is the company insured for liability and workers compensation in Manitoba?
  • Do they have ISA-certified arborists on staff or on-site?
  • What does the quote include — chipping, hauling, stump grinding?
  • Will they handle permits if required by the City of Winnipeg for boulevard trees?
  • What is the estimated time to complete the job?

Being informed going in makes the entire experience smoother and ensures you get fair value for what is often a significant home maintenance expense.
