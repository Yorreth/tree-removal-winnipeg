Trimming & Pruning

Tree Trimming & Pruning in Winnipeg

Regular trimming does more than keep a tree looking tidy — it removes weak or dead limbs before they fail, improves airflow and sunlight through the canopy, and can add years to a tree's life. It also matters for safety: overgrown branches near a roofline, power line, or walkway are one storm away from becoming a problem.

Signs your tree needs trimming

  • Dead or hanging deadwood in the canopy
  • Branches rubbing the roof, siding, or overhead lines
  • A canopy that's grown noticeably lopsided or too dense
  • Broken or cracked limbs left over from a storm
  • Old cuts that were never shaped properly and are now regrowing poorly

Timing matters in Winnipeg

Most trees are best pruned during late fall or winter dormancy, when cuts heal faster and it's easier to see the branch structure without leaves in the way. American elms are the exception: Manitoba restricts all elm pruning from April 1 to July 31, since fresh cuts attract the elm bark beetles that spread Dutch Elm Disease. If you're not sure whether your tree falls under that rule, see our guide on Dutch Elm Disease in Winnipeg.

Suspect Dutch Elm Disease rather than routine deadwood? Don't prune it — report it to 311 first. Pruning a DED-infected elm outside of removal can spread the disease faster.

Our pruning process

  1. Assessment. A crew looks at the tree's species, health, and growth pattern to plan cuts that support long-term health, not just a quick trim.
  2. Selective pruning. Deadwood, crossing branches, and hazard limbs are removed first, followed by shaping cuts.
  3. Cleanup. All cut branches and debris are hauled away, leaving the yard as clean as it started.

Common questions

Most mature trees do well with a trim every 2–3 years. Younger trees benefit from more frequent, lighter shaping to guide healthy structure as they grow.

Crews can handle branches near service lines to your home, but work near Manitoba Hydro's main distribution lines is typically coordinated with the utility directly for safety.

Correct pruning, done at the right time of year with clean cuts, is healthy for the tree. Problems usually come from over-pruning or cutting at the wrong time — both of which a licensed crew will avoid.
